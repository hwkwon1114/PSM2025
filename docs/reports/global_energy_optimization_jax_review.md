# Fixed-final-energy optimization and JAX: decision memo

**Review/search date: 2026-09-23.** Decision synthesis complete; live software-freshness and some paper full-text verification remain incomplete (limits below). This is a numerical/software review, **not** the deferred forming/metastability review. The older mechanics review's historical frame and IDs are unchanged.

## Decision

**Do not replace HLBFGS wholesale or authorize a full JAX rewrite yet.** Retain the absolute-stop, independently residual-checked, energy-corrected L-BFGS implementation as comparator. A safeguarded same-load restart is a proposed addition supported by the isolated diagnostic; it is **not yet integrated into the production continuation solver**. Add a small, reproducible search over distinct initial configurations of the **same final discrete energy**. Investigate a structure-aware second-order challenger only with a materially different derivative/linear-solve cost model from the unsuccessful existing TR run. JAX is a plausible **energy/gradient/HVP throughput tool**, not a cure for poor conditioning or a global optimizer.

The target remains the global minimum over the stated admissible configurations, but none of the proposed finite-budget methods certifies it. Report **“lowest-energy solution found”**, alongside acceptance, stability diagnostics, budget and failures.

| Decision layer | Recommendation | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Energy/derivative implementation and platform | Prototype the frozen float64 element energy in JAX; compare with existing analytic C++ gradient and TinyAD HVP. Retain native sparse/cached alternatives. | AD/JIT/GPU support does not imply faster end-to-end solves, better basins, or correct ported physics. |
| Local convergence | Retain corrected/restarted L-BFGS; conditionally compare sparse modified/projected Newton or properly preconditioned Newton-CG. | Restart, tighter tolerance and local quadratic convergence do not escape a stable minimum. |
| Basin/global search | Spend a bounded budget on structured multistart first; later consider basin hopping or a few homotopies, always finishing at the identical final objective. | Several minima, a positive local Hessian, or repeated agreement are not global certificates. |
| Admissibility | Freeze constraints and configuration domain; inspect geometry and objective validity before comparing energies. | An apparently lower energy in a changed domain, degenerate mesh or altered constitutive energy is not an improvement on the same problem. |

### Ranked practical stacks [PROPOSAL]

1. **Native C++ analytic energy/gradient + corrected/restarted HLBFGS + six structured starts.** Lowest implementation risk; strongest project-specific evidence. Use the unchanged physical gradient gates and at most one predeclared near-gate restart. Do not generalize the restart result to iteration-cap failures. This should be the reference, not the algorithm to discard before comparison.
2. **Native energy + TinyAD sparse/cached derivatives + safeguarded modified Newton.** Assemble/cache the base-state sparse Hessian once per outer iteration; use a scale-aware shift or element projection for a descent model and a sparse factorization, testing every step against the original energy. Reuse symbolic sparsity work when valid. If factorization fill/memory is prohibitive, consider truncated Newton-CG with a genuinely coupled block/patch preconditioner, not merely the existing two-scale diagonal. This is a conditional challenger, not another unmodified long TR rerun.
3. **JAX float64 JIT/vectorized element oracle + Optimistix L-BFGS; optional explicit Lineax operator-based linear solves.** Only promote after parity and timing gates. Start on CPU and an identified FP64-capable GPU where available; use microbatches for starts. A Newton-CG/TR outer algorithm with indefinite-curvature safeguards remains engineering work—ordinary Lineax CG alone is not that algorithm. JAX-FEM is a useful backend example, not a demonstrated drop-in port of this director shell. Avoid JAXopt as a new maintained dependency [S2].

The restarted baselines and six-start search above are proposed benchmark configurations, not already implemented production capabilities. All three stacks need the same basin-search wrapper to address the user's global-energy objective. A faster local solver matters primarily if it buys more accepted starts or more useful search within the same budget.

### Correction from archived Newton logs

**Local-record audit, after the literature handoff:** the 2026-08-19 sensitivity report interpreted identical exact-Newton results at `tol=1e-10/1e-12/1e-14` as a converged fixed point in a higher-energy basin. Raw logs contradict that convergence interpretation. Across all three meshes, **all nine such runs say `outer=100 ... (stopped)`**, with reported residuals above even `1e-10`:

| Mesh parameter | Tolerance settings audited | Outer iterations, each | Reported final gradient, each |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| .012 | 1e-10, 1e-12, 1e-14 | 100, stopped | 4.856677e-10 |
| .008 | 1e-10, 1e-12, 1e-14 | 100, stopped | 4.963839e-10 |
| .0057 | 1e-10, 1e-12, 1e-14 | 100, stopped | 5.169959e-10 |

The remaining six loose-tolerance runs report convergence after zero iterations (`1e-6`) or 2–3 iterations (`1e-8`); they do not establish tight equilibrium accuracy. Identical results when changing a tolerance below the residual reached by a fixed iteration budget are **not tolerance-convergence evidence**.

For example, at mesh .012 and nominal `tol=1e-12`, the saved table/logs give exact Newton **E=3.10546e-11, max|z|=2.288 mm, 514 s**, versus HLBFGS **E=2.40430e-11, max|z|=20.948 mm, 588 s**. These are observed endpoints and costs, **not a comparison of equally converged optima**. The HLBFGS log itself reports residual **1.036784e-11**, not a verified modern tight-gate endpoint. Historical `-tol` semantics are not interchangeable across algorithms, and a raw `MINIMUM` label from the old Hessian diagnostic does not override a failed stationarity check or prove global optimality. The exact historical Newton-specific stopping implementation was not recovered in this audit; the **100-iteration stopped records** are directly verified.

**Revised takeaway:** the tried Newton recipe did not demonstrate better accepted energy or reliable convergence, and the separate matrix-free TR configuration was prohibitively slow. We cannot conclude that converged Newton intrinsically chooses a worse local minimum, nor that all Newton methods should be rejected. Any new challenger must first fix/verify its stopping and iteration controls and materially change the derivative/linear-solve cost model; do not simply rerun the same configuration or assume a JAX port changes those facts. Warm-start Newton polishing was suggested historically but its benefit has not been verified here.

### Existing code makes a targeted improvement preferable to a rewrite

[`TinyADHessian_Bilayer.hpp`](../src/libshell/TinyADHessian_Bilayer.hpp), functions `gradient`, `hessianVectorProduct`, `assembleHessian`, already provides exact AD derivatives of the implemented isotropic bilayer kernel. **Code observation:** each HVP recomputes full dense **21×21 element Hessians** over all faces, as well as topology/geometry setup, before multiplying by the local vector. It is globally matrix-free, but not a directional-AD implementation that avoids those element Hessians. Caching base-state element matrices or assembling a sparse matrix can amortize repeated Krylov products; JAX forward-over-reverse HVP is another possibility. Which wins is **unmeasured**.

[`ShellEquilibriumSolver.hpp`](../src/libshell/ShellEquilibriumSolver.hpp) already contains boundary-aware rigid projection, Steihaug negative-curvature handling, TR rollback, diagonal/block-scale metrics, and Ritz-pair residual checks. `blockDiagonalMetric` is two scalar scales; `jacobiMetric` is an absolute diagonal. Neither is a coupled coordinate/director block solve. Defaults such as `sqrt(||g||)` in inner forcing use the physical gradient magnitude, so tiny dimensional scales can request unnecessarily strict inner work; inspect actual options before attributing the old timeout to this. More suitable inner forcing may reduce work, **without changing the final physical gradient gate**. Lanczos is currently diagnostic, not basin injection or a preconditioner.

## Local structure, global search and admissibility

**Modified/projected Newton.** TinyAD's primary API separates original value/gradient from a per-element eigenvalue-modified Hessian [N1]. On the free/gauge-fixed space, an SPD direction model `B` gives `gᵀp < 0` for `Bp = −g`. This is a linear-algebra inference, not convexification of the original shell objective. Use original-energy line search/TR acceptance; do not use the projected matrix to certify stability. Blindly copying TinyAD's default projection floor `1e-9` is inappropriate without scale analysis. Overprojection can destroy useful curvature and slow convergence; the recent PPN abstract explicitly reports this concern [N5], but its methods/results were not fully inspected and no speedup is transferred here.

**Useful preconditioning, not just a new name.** [PROPOSAL] Partition a regularized model into coordinate/director blocks, or use overlapping element patches. Approximate inverses of the diagonal blocks should retain membrane/bending structure; an approximate Schur complement can account for coupling. Require an SPD preconditioner for CG-type methods and remove compatible rigid modes consistently. A scalar Laplacian or two-scale diagonal is not automatically spectrally equivalent to this nonlinear shell tangent. Setup, application, refresh cost and Krylov iterations must all improve walltime. The inspected literature/code supports the architecture, **not a verified shell-specific block-preconditioner performance claim** [N1,N2]. Sparse factorization can avoid many expensive HVPs but may lose on fill-in/memory.

**Saddles are not stable minima.** Truncated CG can detect negative curvature encountered in its Krylov space [project code; N2 abstract supports the trust-region/PCG foundation]. At an exactly stationary saddle, gradient-seeded CG may start with zero and miss all negative modes; a separately checked mode/perturbation is needed. At a strict stable local minimum there is no infinitesimal negative-curvature escape. Restarting L-BFGS or changing its update cannot remove the surrounding energy barrier. Finite structured perturbations, nonmonotone outer moves or changed-objective homotopies are distinct search mechanisms.

**Small outer search.** Wales–Doye map each proposal to a locally minimized energy before outer Monte Carlo search [N3, primary Methods/Results inspected]. This supports the separation of local minimization from basin exploration—not a success guarantee at 32k DOFs. Start with deterministic long-wave bending/director perturbations and sign pairs rather than independent noise in every variable. If later using basin hopping, retain the best accepted endpoint even when accepting a higher-energy intermediate basin. A few eigenstrain/thickness/regularization homotopies are possible **search proposals only**: remove every modification, restore the exact final fields and constraints, then reoptimize/rank by the original energy. Their costs count; no trajectory has privileged physical status for this task.

**Do not substitute Projective Dynamics.** Its paper uses specially designed potentials for alternating projection/global solves [N4]. An exact requisite squared-distance/projection representation of this shell's nonlinear metric, mixed bilayer and director/dihedral terms has **not** been established. The prior child's detailed PD equation transcription was not independently verified from its PDF. There is therefore no basis for assuming the fast constant global system applies unchanged. A PD-like approximation may be a preconditioner/proposal, but cannot silently replace the energy being ranked.

**Why not Adam or generic derivative-free search by default?** [INFERENCE] Adam's coordinatewise moment scaling does not resolve the shell's off-diagonal stiffness coupling, enforce high-accuracy stationarity or certify lower basins. Its usefulness in stochastic learning is not evidence here; subsampling faces would change the exact oracle unless explicitly controlled. High-dimensional generic populations, simplex searches or dense-covariance adaptation can consume many costly evaluations/storage while ignoring known gradients and locality. Low-dimensional derivative-free search over structured perturbation amplitudes is a more plausible outer component. Neither family is ruled out universally; neither has project-specific evidence of superiority.

**Admissibility is a separate gate.** Freeze actual boundary constraints, director conventions, topology, target tensors and configuration domain. Check finite energy/derivatives, nondegenerate faces/edges, constraint residuals and any existing contact/orientation restrictions. No self-contact barrier is visible in the inspected per-face kernel; this is not a whole-project contact audit. If self-intersections are permitted by the stated model, flag them rather than silently excluding them; adding nonintersection constraints/contact changes the optimization problem and requires an explicit decision. Proper rigid alignment removes translations/rotations, not reflections or material relabeling. Assess true-Hessian local stability separately: a negative admissible Rayleigh direction is evidence against a minimum; a positive approximate smallest Ritz value—even with a small eigenpair residual—alone does not certify absence of an unobserved negative mode, much less global optimality.

**Material benefit is plausible but unproved.** JAX may reduce repeated element/HVP cost or increase starts per hour; it cannot by itself improve a fixed local algorithm's basin coverage. Analytic C++ gradients may already be competitive. Compile cost, irregular scatter/reduction, small batches, FP64 hardware limitations, host synchronization and preconditioner quality can erase GPU gains. The benchmark must decide, not generic accelerator claims.

## One next benchmark proposal — no execution authorized here

**A frozen-energy coarse-mesh prototype, not a full port.** [PROPOSAL] A single screening budget of **2 hours cumulative runtime**, including compilation/parity/profiling/checkpoint checks and all failed solves; at most **six starts × three local stacks**, **5 minutes per local solve**, with unused time not triggering extra starts or retries. These are proposed bounds for separate approval, not an extension of earlier jobs. If parity/setup exhausts the budget, stop with a partial result.

1. **Freeze and serialize inputs.** Choose one exact final discrete field set; hash topology, top/bottom metrics, reference curvature, material/thickness, constraints and admissibility rules. Use identical full-float64 coordinate **and director** states, never reconstructed float32 VTP endpoints. Predeclare six states: nominal initialization, one available full-state deformed seed, and sign pairs of two finite long-wave coordinate/director perturbations. Validate geometry before solving. A seed captured at intermediate loading is allowed as an initialization, not mislabeled a final equilibrium; every solve uses the same final fields. No fine-mesh or loading-discretization sweep.
2. **Parity before optimization.** On flat, curved and available near-gate states, compare original energy components, free gradients and HVPs with JAX float64; inspect symmetry/directional consistency and branch/constraint handling. An archived near-gate state needs its exact captured field for that oracle-only check; it need not remain near-gate under the final field used for all optimization comparisons. Use combined absolute/relative errors, not relative error near zero. Require gradient discrepancy comfortably below the tight gate (e.g. `0.1 × 1e-13` for the original free-gradient norm at near-stationary states); predeclare scale-aware HVP and energy tolerances. Energy discrepancies must be below the differences later claimed meaningful. No port passes merely because AD runs.
3. **Separate platform from local algorithm.** First measure identical-state E/g/HVP calls, including cached/assembled native HVP alternatives. Then compare A: corrected/restarted HLBFGS, B: one specified native assembled/cached modified-Newton challenger, C: JAX oracle + one specified limited-memory solver. Only implement the minimum prototype needed; do not substitute the old unchanged TR as a “new” B. Different library line searches/updates make A-versus-C a **whole-stack comparison**, not isolated JAX speed evidence. Fix threads/devices and record versions, memory, compilation and transfer costs. Synchronize accelerator timing.
4. **Keep acceptance invariant.** Record time/evaluations to the `1e-11` gate and, within the same cap, the `1e-13` gate. Compare each gate separately. If using `q = S y`, `Ê = E/E₀`, then `g_y = Sᵀg/E₀`; a scaled residual cannot replace original `g`. Recompute the unscaled free analytic gradient at returned states; retain projected/scaled residuals only as additional diagnostics. Keep rejection codes, nonfinite trials, OOM/compile failures, caps and timeouts. No success-only timing averages.
5. **Two readouts.** Local: per-start acceptance, E/g/HVP and linear-solve counts, residual curves and walltime. Global-search screening: best **accepted original energy** versus cumulative budget across the same six starts, with geometry/director comparisons, symmetry-aware deduplication and original-Hessian stability diagnostics. Retain accepted-but-stability-unresolved states with that label. Tiny energy gaps at the observed reduction-noise scale are unresolved ties, not new minima. Report compilation separately and also include it in cold/end-to-end totals.
6. **Resumability before any subsequent long run.** Test interruption/restart of a full state: exact fields/DOFs/constraints, optimizer history/scales and pending line-search or accepted-boundary state, TR/preconditioner state or reproducible rebuild rules, RNG/outer-search state, counters and consumed walltime. State hashes and resumed residual/energy must agree within declared reproducibility limits. An optimizer-array capture without physical fields is insufficient. A resume consumes the **remaining original cumulative budget**, never a fresh allowance.

**Promotion/kill criterion:** no full JAX port unless parity passes and it delivers a predeclared material improvement (for example, ≥2× warm end-to-end throughput with compilation break-even inside the intended workload, without worse acceptance) or demonstrably better best accepted energy within the same budget. Lower E/g kernel time alone is insufficient. Stop the Newton branch if factorization/preconditioner setup and solve costs do not beat the retained baseline on this screen. A failed small screen is useful evidence; no automatic long rerun follows.
